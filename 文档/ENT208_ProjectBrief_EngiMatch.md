## Section 1 - The Problem

Chinese engineering undergraduates who plan to apply for UK taught Master's programmes often face a fragmented and stressful preparation process. Entry requirements are distributed across many university websites, and the information is rarely standardised. Students must compare GPA expectations, accepted academic backgrounds, English language thresholds, prerequisite modules, required application materials, application systems, deadlines, and special notes such as ATAS or visa-related conditions. Even when the information is public, it is still difficult for a student to judge whether their own profile truly fits a programme, especially when universities use broad phrases such as "related discipline", "strong quantitative background", or "relevant technical experience".

This problem matters because application decisions are costly and time-sensitive. A poor judgement can lead to wasted application fees, unrealistic school choices, weak positioning, or missed opportunities for better-targeted programmes. The problem is especially serious for students who do not have direct access to reliable advisors and therefore rely on online forums, social media, classmates, or commercial agencies. These alternatives are often inconsistent, expensive, or not personalised to the student’s real transcript and language profile.

The difficulty also extends beyond programme selection. Many students are unsure how to present themselves in a UK postgraduate application CV, which usually requires clearer structure, more quantified achievements, and stronger evidence of technical suitability than a typical domestic student CV. They may also struggle to plan the wider process, including when to prepare language tests, internships, documents, finances, accommodation, visa tasks, and departure arrangements. As a result, students do not just need a search tool. They need an integrated system that supports evaluation, preparation, planning, and information confidence.

## Section 2 - Target Users

EngiMatch is designed for a realistic user such as a third-year or final-year Chinese engineering undergraduate who wants to apply for UK Master's programmes in fields like mechanical engineering, electrical engineering, aerospace, robotics, energy systems, or computing-related engineering. This student may already have grades, some internship or project experience, and a language test plan, but still feels uncertain about which universities are realistic, competitive, or too risky. They are likely managing several possible application directions at once and may want to compare different profiles or strategies before making final decisions.

This user cares about efficiency, clarity, and credibility. They want advice that is more personalised than social media content and more affordable than relying fully on a study-abroad agency. They also want help improving their CV in a way that is specific to UK taught Master's applications rather than general job-hunting. A second target user group is staff or advisors who maintain programme data, verify parsed information, and keep the recommendation base trustworthy over time. Their needs are different from students, but they are essential to the quality and sustainability of the platform.

## Section 3 - The Solution

EngiMatch is a web-based platform that supports the UK engineering Master's application journey in a connected workflow instead of treating each task separately. From the student’s perspective, the platform allows them to create one or more applicant profiles by entering their academic background, GPA, language scores, target tracks, and completed courses. The system then compares this information against stored programme requirements and returns clear matching results. Rather than giving only a simple score, it classifies outcomes such as eligible, borderline, and not eligible, and explains the reasons in plain language based on academic fit, language fit, and prerequisite coverage.

The platform also supports decision-making after matching. Students can review results in a dedicated dashboard, compare multiple applicant profiles, and explore recommendations that help separate ambitious, realistic, and safer options. In practice, this is useful because many students consider more than one target field or want to test how changes in GPA, language scores, or course evidence affect their outcomes.

EngiMatch extends beyond matching into application preparation. The built-in AI resume assistant guides users through a structured process: choosing an application direction, uploading or pasting CV content, reviewing extracted sections, receiving diagnosis on structure and relevance, comparing rewritten versions, and saving a final version. This is paired with local rule-based checks and AI-generated suggestions so that students get both instant baseline feedback and more detailed improvement support.

The wider ecosystem of the product covers planning and practical readiness as well. A timeline module helps students organise key tasks across preparation, application, visa, pre-departure, and arrival stages, while also allowing personal study-year and graduation-year settings. A profile page stores key personal settings and account information. In addition, a budget calculator helps users estimate tuition, living costs, visa-related fees, deposits, emergency funds, exchange-rate impact, and available funds. This makes the system more realistic because students often need financial planning alongside academic decision-making.

Finally, EngiMatch includes staff and admin workspaces that support long-term data quality. Staff can create universities, add or edit programmes, identify stale records, and review items needing attention. Admin and staff users can also crawl programme pages, parse requirement data, compare raw source text against structured fields, and manually verify records before they are trusted by students. This dual student-facing and staff-facing design is an important part of the product, because accurate recommendations depend on well-maintained underlying data.

### Key Features

1. Applicant profile creation with academic background, GPA, language scores, target directions, and completed courses.
2. Multi-profile dashboard so users can manage several application scenarios and compare matching outcomes.
3. Automated programme matching with plain-language explanations of eligibility, borderline status, and missing requirements.
4. AI-assisted university recommendation to help users build a more balanced shortlist.
5. AI resume assistant with upload, section review, diagnosis, optimisation, and final-version saving.
6. Timeline planning tools linked to study year and graduation year, helping users manage preparation milestones.
7. Budget calculator covering tuition, living expenses, visa costs, deposits, exchange rates, and funding gaps.
8. Bilingual Chinese-English interface for both guidance and application-related content.
9. Staff and admin workspaces for creating, editing, reviewing, and verifying university and programme records.
10. Data crawling and verification flow so programme information can be checked against source text before use.

## Section 4 - Success Metrics

EngiMatch will be considered successful if it improves both student usability and data reliability. First, at least 80% of test users should be able to complete an applicant profile and receive matching results without external help. Second, at least 70% of users should report that the matching explanation is clearer and more actionable than their previous manual comparison process. Third, at least 60% of users who upload a CV should continue to the diagnosis or optimisation stage, showing that the AI resume workflow creates real value instead of being abandoned early. Fourth, in testing, students should be able to build a shortlist and basic financial estimate faster than when using university websites and spreadsheets alone. Fifth, users of the timeline module should be able to identify the next major preparation step based on their current study year. Finally, staff users should be able to review and maintain programme records with fewer visible data errors and fewer unverified entries before those records are used in student-facing matching results.
